# Development basis and roadmap

This document summarises the owner-provided **Nido del Bosque — Interactive Architectural Design Studio** prompt and **Nido del Bosque Development Brief V1.0**, dated October 2026. They provide product requirements and concept context; they do not establish engineering, legal or financial approval. The original confidential PDF and its images are not copied into the repository.

## Project intent

Explore a small, private, design-led cabin for two guests in Santa Elena, Medellín, Colombia, with construction targeted for 2027 after feasibility and approvals. The preferred direction is a contemporary rectangular elevated volume with warm timber, dark metal, generous forest-facing glazing, a single-slope roof and independent access.

The adjacent Airbnb does not establish operating permission for the new cabin. Tourism registration, guest reporting, insurance and operating requirements need local review. The application does not forecast rental income or investment returns.

## Starter scope

The initial application focuses on design exploration:

1. A calm, responsive studio interface in warm off-white, forest green, charcoal and timber tones.
2. A procedural 3D elevated cabin with orbit/zoom, preset cameras, four exterior palettes and controls for the principal dimensions and roof pitch.
3. A corresponding indicative 2D floor plan and live gross-area allowance calculation.
4. An editable 14-category COP budget with a fixed COP 80M ceiling and clearly identified placeholder allocations.
5. A local inspiration gallery with categories and notes.
6. Browser-local persistence for the current baseline design, budget and images.

The original brief baseline, **Contemporary Elevated Cabin — V1**, was a 9 m × 6 m enclosed mass plus a 3 m × 2 m terrace, totalling 60 m². The owner’s V2 refinement moves the stairs and entrance to the left side (when facing the front) and extends the front balcony across the full cabin length. Keeping the 9 m × 6 m cabin and 2 m balcony depth gives 54 m² enclosed, 18 m² balcony and approximately 2.88 m² side landing: 74.88 m² overall, before any additional stair classification. This exceeds the reported allowance and requires a revised area strategy; the app makes that overage visible. Illustrative 150 mm walls and internal partition deductions produce an estimated net quantity; neither this deduction nor the procedural geometry is engineering-verified.

The budget categories distribute the fixed ceiling for exploration. The reference brief describes a broader reverse-budget framework with COP 8M contingency and a severely constrained building remainder. Neither framework demonstrates that the preferred elevated cabin can be delivered for COP 80M. Replace placeholders with comparable, itemised quotations before using totals for a decision.

## Deliberately deferred

- Survey-based site planning, terrain, boundary geometry and positioning against the neighbouring property.
- Independent room editing, window sizing/positioning, staircase relocation and coordinated interior construction detail.
- Named design versions, multiple budget scenarios, side-by-side comparisons and export/import backups.
- Detailed furnishing and interior walkthroughs.
- Cloud accounts, cross-device synchronisation and collaborative review.
- Engineering analysis, permission classification, supplier pricing and financial-return forecasts.

The uploaded PDF's concept imagery, floor plans and satellite annotations are references only. Their geometry must not be treated as surveyed site data or copied into an authoritative construction model.

## Decisions before construction

Obtain the written municipal permission and clarify how parking, stairs, elevation and any future accessible roof affect the allowance. Commission the appropriate survey and ground/structural review. Compare the full elevated concept with reduced and ground-level alternatives using local quotations, preserving contingency and essential safety requirements. Confirm independent access, utility capacity, wastewater arrangements and tourism operating obligations with qualified local professionals.

The product should help organise those decisions. It must not present an illustrative model, owner-reported allowance or allocated budget as approval to build.
